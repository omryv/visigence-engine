# Play now and creator props

Visigames by Visigence opens a game launcher with a featured mission, world catalog, editable seed and personal best. Arcade (a sunny pastel village with critter enemies and fantasy weapons, drawn toon-shaded) is the catalog entry. Entries carry their own art, crop, lighting and accent metadata; the responsive catalog has no fixed three-card layout. Worlds describe environments, not a fixed list of future gameplay modes. Additional gameplay modes still require their own engine rules and mission presets.

Generated environmental PNG artwork and custom SVG brand/control assets load from R2 in the studio and are bundled locally in exported games. Concept art is labelled as such. The same visual identity appears in the menu, world thumbnails, loading overlay, results and 1200×630 downloadable challenge card. Remix opens the existing graph for the selected world; returning to Play preserves its edited mission. Legacy project files, local saves, imported bytes and records remain readable; new project downloads use `.visigames.json`.

Results show the same mission card, elapsed simulation time, replay, PNG card download and a Beat this link. Personal bests are local to the browser and keyed by seed plus gameplay rules, including imported props. Graphics and graph positions do not split records. Shared links encode the resolved rules; the same seed alone is not a claim that different blueprints play identically. Missions containing local imported files must be shared as a game ZIP; the UI explains this when a player tries to copy a hosted link.

## Static GLB import

Use **My assets** or **Import prop** under Creator tools, drop or choose a self-contained GLB, inspect its rotating preview, set the height and solid-box/decorative collision, and supply creator/source/licence details plus a redistribution declaration. The Blender supply case is an original sample generated by `tools/blender/build_import_demo.py` through the supplied bridge session. The bridge script restores the previous selection and does not save or replace the user's scene.

Limits: 8 MB and 50,000 triangles per prop; 8 props, 32 MB and 100,000 triangles per project; 16 materials, 12 embedded PNG/JPEG textures (up to 2048px) and 256 nodes per file. Decoded texture memory is capped at 64 MB per prop. Objects are grounded and centered, limited to 4m on every axis, and placed in clear spaces near the starting route. Original materials are preserved. Use **Place props** to drag on the compiled world map or enter X/Z position, yaw and height. The compiler rejects overlaps with world geometry, other props and reserved mission areas before saving. Props remain grounded. Solid collision uses a conservative axis-aligned box around the rotated prop; concave mesh collision, animation, skins and compressed GLBs remain outside this stage.

GLB bytes are stored in IndexedDB by SHA-256. Project metadata and rights declarations are stored in project JSON. JSON is not a binary asset container: opening it on another device requires reimporting the original GLB. Game ZIPs include the exact original bytes under `imports/` and `IMPORTED-ASSET-CREDITS.txt`; they load without IndexedDB or the asset origin. This credits file covers the user's imported props, not the deferred engine-wide commercial licence/provenance work.

## Creator identity

**Mission identity** accepts a studio name, accent, mission artwork and studio logo. PNG/JPEG files are limited to 4 MB and 4096 pixels per side, decoded before acceptance, and require creator/licence information and redistribution permission. The artwork appears on the mission card, start/results screen and PNG share card; exports include the exact files and their credits. Custom local files require sharing the complete game ZIP. Cosmetic identity changes do not reset personal bests.

## Readability and feedback

Enemy materials get view-dependent emissive edges: cool against amber dawn, pale against dusk. Authored eye/visor materials use a consistent theme tint. The effect works in the lean renderer without extra lights or stronger post-processing. Attack windup increases the enemy rim cue. Hits, headshots, kills, checkpoint completion, and mission outcomes receive quiet confirmation tones that respect sound settings. Crosshair feedback distinguishes hits, headshots, kills and the final area clear. The damage wash was softened. ADS now applies its sensitivity multiplier in the fixed simulation step, and the start/pause screen exposes a personal sensitivity slider. Phone controls add hold-to-aim and larger fire/move controls; releasing a touch look gesture no longer cancels another finger's fire input.

## Validation

`node tools/test/run.mjs` includes the player-experience suite for deterministic prop placement, solid/decorative collision, mission identity, import budgets, malformed/animated/external-resource rejection and ZIP byte/credit integrity. Browser verification covers the generated deck, phone layout, Blender sample preview, persistent import and real GPU mission generation. Hardware-specific sustained mobile frame rate and real-phone multitouch still need device testing.
